## Shared exact-edition reading

Germany changed during the tournament rather than holding one fixed shape. Lahm began in central midfield and returned to right-back from the quarter-final. Schweinsteiger grew into a deeper controlling role, Kroos set the rhythm from left of centre, and Khedira supplied the strongest forward running from central midfield. Özil started from the left but moved inside to create.

Höwedes played every minute at left-back despite being a centre-back, giving the back line a narrower and more defensive left side. Hummels combined first-ball defending with a major set-piece threat. Klose gave the attack a reference centre-forward; Götze and Schürrle offered mobile, match-changing alternatives.

## Card record

### André Schürrle — RESEARCH / PORTRAIT

Before:

> To follow Schürrle, begin with staying connected to the move after releasing the ball. He uses a short pass to bring a third teammate into the move. The same thread continues in another phase when he moves after passing so the receiver still has close support.

Final:

> Schürrle changes matches by attacking space at full speed from the left. Off the bench he improvises the extra-time opener against Algeria, scores twice against Brazil and drives past his man to cross for Götze's winner in the final.

Evidence and judgment: FIFA's Schürrle retrospective and the official match record support the substitute role, three goals and final assist. The final replaces an interchangeable support-pass sequence with the direct running and repeated bench impact that defined his tournament.

Limit: “changes matches” summarizes the documented substitute contributions; it is not a claim that every appearance produced the same effect.

### Bastian Schweinsteiger — PROVE / ROLE_GUIDE

Before:

> For Schweinsteiger, start with making the first pass after a recovery easier than the tackle. Look for how he uses one touch to secure the ball and the next to release it. That pattern continues when he returns the ball and changes angle with the direct route crowded.

Final:

> Schweinsteiger grows into the tournament after being eased through the group stage, then becomes the deeper controller when Germany reshape for the quarter-final. From the base of midfield, his positioning protects the centre and the direction of his passing sets the pace.

Evidence and judgment: line-ups, the technical report and contemporary midfield analysis establish his managed group-stage use and deeper knockout role. The final keeps a restrained role guide while replacing unproved two-touch mechanics with the tournament's real progression.

Limit: “deeper controller” compresses a shared midfield responsibility. It does not erase his physical leadership or claim that Kroos and Lahm stopped contributing to control.

### Benedikt Höwedes — RESEARCH / ROLE_GUIDE

Before:

> The first thing to track with Höwedes is waiting for the loose touch before committing to the challenge. Notice how he slows the attack long enough for the defensive shape to recover. Beyond that, he stays goal-side until support is close enough for him to engage.

Final:

> Höwedes is a centre-back used at left-back for every minute of Germany's title run. He stays narrower and more defensive than a conventional overlapping full-back, yet remains an aerial target at corners and hits the post with a header in the final.

Evidence and judgment: official line-ups and FIFA's retrospective establish his position and complete minutes; the final record establishes the header against the post. The final explains an unusual left-back role instead of assigning generic centre-back challenge mechanics.

Limit: “narrower and more defensive” is a tactical synthesis from his natural position and observed use, not a claim derived from a published heat-map comparison.

### Mario Götze — RESEARCH / PORTRAIT

Evidence and judgment: FIFA's final-goal reconstruction and the tournament line-ups support both his mobile attacking use and the exact decisive sequence. The final connects the goal to his role rather than reducing his edition to a stock forward-runner card.

### Mats Hummels — RESEARCH / PORTRAIT

### Mesut Özil — RESEARCH / ROLE_GUIDE

Before:

### Philipp Lahm — PROVE / PORTRAIT

Before:

Evidence and judgment: the official line-ups and contemporary analysis establish the four midfield starts and move to right-back. The final preserves the old card's intelligent overlap but places it inside the edition's defining positional change.

### Toni Kroos — EDITION_SPLIT / PORTRAIT

Before:

## Blind comparison and correction pass

Two independent reviewers received randomized old-versus-proposed pairs with exact-edition evidence constraints and no old/new labels. Both preferred all ten proposed replacements. Neuer and Müller were held out as controls because research upheld `KEEP`.

The team-level read then produced three narrow corrections:

- Hummels's first proposal began “gives Germany a centre-back,” repeating Götze's nearby “gives Germany” construction and stating his position before his identity. Two reviewers preferred the direct final opening.
- Höwedes's first proposal ended “heading against the post.” Two reviewers preferred the idiomatic “hits the post with a header.”
- Khedira's first proposal said “Germany's central three.” One reviewer valued that specificity and one rejected its implication of a fixed shape. A fresh three-way blind comparison ranked the final “central midfield” wording above both “midfield” and “central three.”

An earlier Klose draft used “inside the box.” It was shortened to “in the box” to stay within the authored-card length contract without changing meaning. No extra sentence was added to any card merely to meet a cadence target.

## Team-level self-critique

The final set now reads as one changing team rather than twelve unrelated templates. Lahm's positional move connects to Schweinsteiger's deeper control; Kroos and Khedira have different central functions; Özil's inward creation relates to the reference point Klose supplies. Schürrle and Götze are both substitutes but no longer sound interchangeable. Hummels and Höwedes are distinguished by first-ball authority versus a narrow converted-full-back role.

The strongest editorial restraint is the two preserved controls. Neuer's high starting position and Müller's movement away from expected zones remain more useful than fact-heavy replacements.

The remaining uncertainty is appropriately bounded:

- Höwedes's narrow, defensive use is a tactical synthesis rather than a published measurement.
- Schweinsteiger's “deeper controller” compresses shared control and omits some of his physical leadership.
- Özil's portrait prioritizes his creative movement over his defensive work from the left.
- Several cards use Brazil or the final because those matches provide the clearest role-proving evidence; the events are not used as interchangeable decoration.

The untouched generated 2014 cards still trigger one archive cadence warning: the `different-phase` family covers 12.4% of generated cards. That warning belongs to cards outside this team sprint and was not expanded into a generator rewrite.

## Final self-critique

Germany 2014 is a clear net improvement and supports moving to the next team-edition. The revisions recover this side's most important editorial fact: its roles changed as the tournament developed. They also demonstrate that an edition portrait can be concise, event-backed and specific without forcing three sentences.

The audit fix was justified because it protects authored translations as the archive grows, but it is also a warning: future team sprints must not casually turn editorial work into infrastructure work. The process remains sound if narrow correctness failures are repaired once and then left alone.

No unresolved process wall requires approval before proceeding. No unrelated portrait, Best XI rationale, UI layout, broad generator system, commit, push or deployment changed.
