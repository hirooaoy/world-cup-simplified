## Shared exact-edition reading

Argentina did not play one fixed shape throughout the tournament. The side used a back four through the group stage and the Uruguay tie, then added Enrique and settled into the three-defender knockout structure associated with the title run. Brown swept behind Ruggeri and Cuciuffo; Batista held the centre; Enrique and Burruchaga supplied balance and running; Valdano stretched the line; Maradona moved freely between midfield and attack.

The principal primary source was FIFA's contemporary technical report:

- [Argentina team report and tournament roles, part 2, PDF pages 19–22](https://web.archive.org/web/20111220052347id_/http://www.fifa.com/mm/document/afdeveloping/technicaldevp/50/09/06/fwc_mexico_1986_en_part2_281.pdf)
- [Argentina tactical study and positional reviews, part 4, PDF pages 1–4, 13, 16, 21 and 23](https://web.archive.org/web/20100614213621id_/http://www.fifa.com/mm/document/afdeveloping/technicaldevp/50/09/00/fwc_mexico_1986_en_part4_279.pdf)
- [Match reports and line-ups, part 1](https://web.archive.org/web/20111220061752id_/http://www.fifa.com/mm/document/afdeveloping/technicaldevp/50/09/08/fwc_mexico_1986_en_part1_283.pdf)

The report pages were text-extracted and visually checked against the rendered scans before they were used.

## Card record

### Claudio Borghi — RESEARCH / ROLE_GUIDE

Before:

> Watch Borghi for moving between centre-back and full-back without being tracked. One detail is how he waits for the defender to look away before accelerating. The idea carries into how he moves wide of the centre-backs to open the middle for a teammate.

Final:

> Borghi’s brief 1986 role is as a young forward dropping toward Maradona and Valdano to combine. He starts against Italy and Bulgaria, but does not feature once the knockout rounds begin.

Evidence: the FIFA technical report records his two group-stage appearances and no knockout minutes. Maradona later described Borghi as a major talent for whom this World Cup may have arrived early. [AFA appearance note](https://www.afa.com.ar/admin/posts/claudio-borghi-campeon-del-mundo-en-1986-cumple-55-anos), [Maradona's tournament memoir](https://lastimaanadiemaestro.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/mexico-86.-mi-mundial-mi-verdad-diego-armando-maradona.pdf).

Why better: it removes three unsupported running mechanics and gives an honest limited-use role. Limit: two appearances do not support a full individual scouting portrait, so this intentionally remains a concise role guide.

### Diego Maradona — RESEARCH / PORTRAIT

Before:

> Maradona's signature is moving the ball forward without forcing the crowded route. Watch how he carries through the open lane if no forward pass is available. Separately, he uses a short pass to bring a third teammate into the move.

Final:

> Maradona plays free between Argentina’s midfield and forwards, receiving where defenders must leave their line to meet him. He carries until several opponents commit, then either continues toward goal himself or releases Valdano and Burruchaga into the space they have left.

Evidence: the FIFA tactical study describes his free movement, close left-footed carrying, changes of pace and direction, ability to draw defenders, and passing vision. Its positional review identifies him as both a creator and finisher. [FIFA dribbling study](https://www.fifa.com/en/tournaments/mens/worldcup/articles/dribbles-diego-maradona-1982-1986-1990-1994).

Why better: it makes the defender-drawing carry and the choice after it the centre of the portrait. The old third-player sentence was generic and unsupported. Limit: published FIFA retrospectives disagree on whether to record four or five assists, so no assist total appears in the generic card.

### Héctor Enrique — RESEARCH / ROLE_GUIDE

Before:

> In Enrique's position, start with shaping the pace of the game from midfield. One sign is how he slows the touch to give teammates time to move ahead of the ball. The same idea appears as he changes his angle before switching play away from pressure.

Final:

> Enrique enters the starting XI when Argentina add a midfielder for the last three matches, balancing the centre beside Batista. He keeps the short passing moving, and his simple ball near halfway begins Maradona’s run against England.

Evidence: exact line-ups show Enrique starting the quarter-final, semi-final and final after the midfield change. Tactical review supports his balancing and linking role; FIFA identifies his pass near halfway as the beginning of Maradona's second goal against England. [FIFA England retrospective](https://www.fifa.com/en/tournaments/mens/worldcup/articles/argentina-england-1986-diego-maradona), [exact-edition tactical review](https://www.holdingmidfield.com/bilardismo-argentina-at-the-1986-world-cup/).

Why better: it replaces unsupported touch-slowing and switching mechanics with his actual knockout role. Limit: the pass begins a long solo run; the card deliberately does not call it an official assist.

### Jorge Burruchaga — EDITION_SPLIT / PORTRAIT

Before:

> Burruchaga is defined by joining the attack from a position defenders cannot watch continuously. One sign is how he arrives near the box after the first line of markers has followed the ball. Separately, he slows the touch to give teammates time to move ahead of the ball.

Final:

> From the left of midfield, Burruchaga covers ground in both directions and bursts beyond the ball when a lane opens. He arrives at the far post to score against Bulgaria, then runs diagonally into the right channel onto Maradona’s pass for the winner in the final.

Evidence: FIFA's technical report calls out his unusually large range from midfield to the penalty area, his defensive responsibility on the left, and his diagonal attacking runs. The exact match reports record the far-post header and final run. [FIFA interview with Burruchaga](https://www.fifa.com/en/tournaments/mens/worldcup/articles/jorge-burruchaga-argentina-interview).

Why better: it separates 1986 from his near-duplicate 1990 card and makes the two-way running identity lead. The final is evidence of that identity rather than the entire portrait.

### Jorge Valdano — KEEP / PORTRAIT, then surgical correction

Before:

> Valdano's edge comes from attacking the space behind defenders before it fully opens. One sign is how he starts his run while the back line is still watching the ball. The same edge returns as he attacks the near-post lane before the marker can turn.

Final:

> Valdano's edge comes from attacking the space behind defenders before it fully opens. One sign is how he starts his run while the back line is still watching the ball. That timing takes him into the left channel for Argentina’s second goal in the final.

Evidence: FIFA's positional review describes his wide range and runs on either side. The final analysis and Valdano's own account describe the early diagonal into the left channel. [AFA first-person account](https://www.afa.com.ar/Sitio/posts/mexico86-mi-gol-en-la-final).

Why better: the strong first two sentences remain untouched; the unsupported near-post example is replaced with the exact 1986 action. Limit: the final sentence is match-specific, but it demonstrates rather than replaces the established movement identity.

### José Luis Brown — TWEAK / ROLE_GUIDE

Before:

> From Brown's position, first look for keeping the back line connected as the attack changes sides. He holds the dangerous lane until a teammate can pressure the ball. Separately, he steps toward the receiver only after the covering defender is set.

Final:

> Brown plays as the sweeper behind Ruggeri and Cuciuffo, moving across to double up when either stopper engages. In the final, he attacks Burruchaga’s free-kick to head the opening goal, then stays on after seriously injuring his shoulder.

Evidence: the FIFA team report identifies Brown as the replacement sweeper behind the two central markers. Exact-edition tactical analysis describes his cover movement. FIFA records the final header, his ever-present tournament and the shoulder injury. [FIFA Brown retrospective](https://inside.fifa.com/news/former-world-champion-jose-luis-brown-passes-away), [FIFA interview with Burruchaga](https://www.fifa.com/en/tournaments/mens/worldcup/articles/jorge-burruchaga-argentina-interview).

Why better: the actual libero relationship replaces reusable centre-back timing. His defining final is included without turning the generic card into a Best XI selection argument. Limit: “seriously injuring his shoulder” follows FIFA's confidence; the card does not add a more specific medical diagnosis.

### Julio Olarticoechea — KEEP / PORTRAIT

Before and final, unchanged:

> Watch Olarticoechea for protecting the far-post runner while the ball stays on the opposite side. First, notice how he protects the inside channel before tracking movement wide. Beyond that, Olarticoechea turns toward his own goal early enough to match the first recovery run.

Evidence: the team report documents his hybrid wide role and place from the Uruguay tie onward. The card closely describes his decisive recovery around Lineker at the far post against England. [Guardian review using FIFA archive footage](https://www.theguardian.com/football/2020/apr/26/world-cup-questions-were-england-robbed-by-argentina-at-mexico-86).

Why KEEP OLD: the existing card is supported, observable and more useful than an event-only rewrite. Its micro-mechanics are an editorial observation aligned with the exact role, not a deduction from position alone.

### Nery Pumpido — EDITION_SPLIT / PORTRAIT

Before:

> Pumpido's edge comes from keeping his decisions calm when the penalty area becomes crowded. You can see it when he catches cleanly where possible and pushes uncertain balls away from traffic. Beyond that, he protects the central lane and forces the attacker toward the harder finish.

Final:

> Pumpido brings steadiness rather than spectacle to Argentina’s goal. Well protected by the defence, he stays involved by organising the line and launching breaks with long throws; a late stop to deny Rubén Paz protects the lead against Uruguay.

Evidence: FIFA's contemporary goalkeeping review says Pumpido was rarely called on, organised his defence continually and launched dangerous breaks with long throws. Maradona's memoir records the late Rubén Paz save. [FIFA interview with Pumpido](https://inside.fifa.com/tournaments/mens/worldcup/qatar2022/news/pumpido-argentina-were-great-team-unique-figure), [Maradona's tournament memoir](https://lastimaanadiemaestro.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/mexico-86.-mi-mundial-mi-verdad-diego-armando-maradona.pdf).

Why better: it distinguishes the complete 1986 title run from Pumpido's near-duplicate injury-shortened 1990 card. The old crowded-area, parry-direction and forced-angle claims were not established by the available evidence. Limit: the primary report also notes uncertainty on high crosses; the final portrait avoids turning either the strength or weakness into an absolute.

### Oscar Ruggeri — KEEP / PORTRAIT

Before and final, unchanged:

> Reading the flight of the ball before the aerial duel begins defines the way Ruggeri plays. Look for how he meets the ball at its highest useful point and directs it away from pressure. Separately, he stays goal-side until support is close enough for him to engage.

Evidence: Ruggeri operated as a stopper with Brown covering and scored a header against Korea Republic. Burruchaga's FIFA interview recalls Ruggeri repeatedly winning aerial contests. [FIFA interview with Burruchaga](https://www.fifa.com/en/tournaments/mens/worldcup/articles/jorge-burruchaga-argentina-interview).

Why KEEP OLD: the aerial identity is both exact and distinctive. Replacing it with the Korea goal or a generic stopper summary would be more literal but less useful.

### Pedro Pasculli — RESEARCH / ROLE_GUIDE

Before:

> With Pasculli in this position, look first for attacking the space behind defenders before it fully opens. He waits for the defender to look away before accelerating. The same thread shows in how he attacks the near-post lane before the marker can turn.

Final:

> Pasculli’s 1986 role was brief but decisive: he partnered Valdano in the opener, then returned against Uruguay. He finished the only goal of the round-of-16 tie after Burruchaga’s cross broke loose in the area.

Evidence: exact line-ups establish the two starts. FIFA's match report records the broken cross and finish. [AFA Korea Republic account](https://www.afa.com.ar/Sitio/posts/mexico86-capitulo-1-corea-del-sur), [AFA Uruguay account](https://www.afa.com.ar/Sitio/posts/mexico86-capitulo-4-uruguay), [FIFA Pasculli interview](https://www.fifa.com/es/articles/pedro-pasculli-entrevista-maradona).

Why better: it removes unsupported look-away acceleration and near-post technique while still giving the user a memorable exact-edition contribution. Limit: the evidence supports a concise contribution guide, not a full personal movement portrait.

### Sergio Batista — PROVE / ROLE_GUIDE

Before:

> In Batista's position, start with protecting the route into the centre before following the ball. One action to notice is how he holds the centre until a teammate recovers behind the ball. Separately, he turns out of pressure only after checking the space behind him.

Final:

> Batista is the interceptor in front of Argentina’s defence and the first passer when possession is won. He holds the central space as teammates advance, then decides whether the next move needs a short pass or a longer ball into space.

Evidence: FIFA's team report calls him the interceptor ahead of the defence, while its positional review says he performed the midfield-libero role excellently. Exact-edition accounts describe his clean first distribution. [FIFA interview with Ricardo Giusti](https://www.fifa.com/en/tournaments/mens/worldcup/articles/ricardo-giusti-argentina-mexico-1986), [Coaches' Voice tactical analysis](https://es.coachesvoice.com/cv/carlos-bilardo-argentina-tactica-maestros/).

Why better: it preserves the supported screening idea but adds the first pass that made the role identifiable. The pressure-turn micro-detail was unsupported.

## Blind comparison

Two independent reviewers received randomized A/B pairs and the exact-edition evidence constraints, without old/new labels. Both selected all nine proposed replacements. Neither reviewer selected an old version or recorded a tie.

Their narrow comments produced four wording corrections before the final comparison:

- Borghi was named explicitly as a young forward.
- Maradona's abstract “next defensive line” became defenders leaving their line to meet him.
- Pumpido's ambiguous “save from Rubén Paz” became a stop to deny him.
- Batista's interceptor identity was moved into the opening sentence.

Two fresh reviewers then compared old versus final with the orientations reversed. Again, both selected all nine final versions with no ties.

Rejected material included the generic third-player action for Maradona, Enrique's unsupported tempo and switching mechanics, Burruchaga's slowed-touch sentence, Valdano's near-post route, Pumpido's parry-direction and angle-forcing claims, Pasculli's look-away acceleration, and Batista's pressure turn.

## Team-level self-critique

The sprint clearly improves factual honesty and edition identity. The old team read as a collection of reusable viewing templates: Borghi and Pasculli shared look-away acceleration; Enrique and Burruchaga shared slowed-touch wording; Brown and Batista shared hold-until-cover logic; Valdano and Pasculli shared the same near-post pattern. The final team has distinct jobs and relationships.

The main risk is the opposite one: several limited-evidence cards now lean on usage and a named match. That is acceptable for Borghi and Pasculli because a short honest contribution guide is their evidence ceiling, but it should not become the default for researched PORTRAIT cards. Maradona, Burruchaga, Brown, Pumpido and Batista still lead with football identity before tournament evidence.

Three mentions of the final are visible across Burruchaga, Valdano and Brown. They describe different actions and are the best exact-edition evidence for each player, so removing them for cadence variety would weaken the copy. This should still be watched when adjacent teams are added.

## Locale review

Only the nine approved English changes were translated. Olarticoechea, Ruggeri and every other archive string were left untouched.

- Spanish uses a Latin American football register, including *mediocampo*, *arco*, *tiro libre* and *fase eliminatoria*. Enrique's role uses *mediocampista* rather than the initially proposed *centrocampista*.
- Korean keeps the football actions compact and natural rather than mirroring English syntax. `Cuciuffo` is rendered `쿠시우포`; this is a minor orthographic uncertainty because the checked-in archive had no established form.
- Chinese uses established football forms in the prose, including 博尔吉, 恩里克, 库休福 and 帕斯库利. Older archives sometimes render Pasculli as 帕斯古力, but 帕斯库利 is the more natural modern form.

No translated card introduced a repeated transition across this team. The four-locale meanings retain the same evidence ceiling: Borghi and Pasculli remain concise role/contribution guides, Enrique's pass is not inflated into an assist, and Brown's shoulder injury is not given an unsupported diagnosis.

The generic-card layer and Best XI layer remain separate. On the 1986 highlights page, selecting Maradona from the champion introduction showed the approved generic portrait, while selecting him from the Best XI showed the tournament-selection rationale.

The following approved generic cards were opened through their natural public triggers:

- Spanish: Burruchaga on the 1986 highlights page.

All visible cards retained the full description, statistics and edition label without clipping or viewport overflow at a 1470 × 836 desktop viewport. The longest inspected Spanish card, Burruchaga, remained readable as one compact card rather than becoming a scrolling analytics panel.

Borghi, Enrique and Pumpido do not have a natural generic-card trigger in the current 1986 page copy: the match summaries expose only a selected subset of confirmed starters, and Pumpido's highlights-page control belongs to the separate Best XI layer. Their exact saved strings, locale mappings and shared card data were verified, but they were not falsely recorded as visually opened in situ. This is a discoverability limitation in the existing UI, not evidence of a prose-rendering defect, and no UI change was made.

One pre-existing locale issue was visible beside the new Chinese prose: Brown's card header uses an awkward generated player-name transliteration while the approved description correctly uses 布朗. Player-name localization was outside this writing sprint and was not changed.

## Final self-critique

Argentina 1986 is a clear net improvement and justifies moving to the next team-edition. All nine accepted changes won both blind comparison rounds; the two strong controls survived untouched; the final team reads as eleven different football jobs rather than a set of rearranged templates.

The sprint also corrected two process assumptions:

1. A Phase A `KEEP` is a strong presumption, not immunity from exact-edition evidence. Valdano required one surgical sentence repair.
2. A `ROLE_GUIDE` can truthfully describe selection pattern and decisive contribution when the evidence cannot sustain detailed personal mechanics.

The main discipline for the next team is to preserve this asymmetry. Iconic or well-documented players may support a true movement portrait; limited-evidence players may end with two honest sentences; an already strong card may remain exactly unchanged. Match events should demonstrate identity where possible, not replace it by default.

No process wall requires approval before the next team. The Chinese header-name inconsistency should be tracked separately from this prose programme rather than allowed to expand the scope of each team sprint.
