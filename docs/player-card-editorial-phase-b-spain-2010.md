## Shared exact-edition reading

Spain's title-winning structure used patient circulation, a double screen formed by Busquets and Alonso, and asymmetric width from the full-backs. Ramos was the more aggressive carrier on the right; Capdevila's advances on the left were more selective. Xavi controlled the circulation, while Iniesta and Villa supplied different kinds of penetration around the central striker.

The team also changed during the tournament. Silva started on the right in the defeat by Switzerland but appeared only once more, for four minutes in the semi-final. Torres occupied the centre-backs and helped create room for Villa attacking from the left, but he remained below full fitness after knee surgery and Pedro replaced him in the starting XI for the semi-final and final.

The principal sources were:

- FIFA's official [2010 technical report](https://web.archive.org/web/20101011115552id_/http://www.fifa.com/mm/document/affederation/technicaldevp/01/29/30/95/reportwm2010_web.pdf), including the Spain formation, appearance and team-analysis pages
- UEFA's reports on the [semi-final](https://www.uefa.com/european-qualifiers/news/0253-0d7c93af1183-aa39d3920e5a-1000--puyol-puts-spain-through-to-final/) and [final](https://www.uefa.com/european-qualifiers/news/0253-0d7c93af1ed8-28be4efeaa14-1000--iniesta-earns-spain-world-cup-crown/)
- AS's contemporary [Spain–Switzerland player analysis](https://as.com/futbol/2010/06/16/mas_futbol/1276639242_850215.html)
- The Guardian's contemporary analysis of [Torres's role](https://www.theguardian.com/football/blog/2010/jun/28/world-cup-2010-fernando-torres-spain), [Torres's fitness](https://www.theguardian.com/football/2010/jul/04/paraguay-spain-world-cup-match-report), and [Spain's final squad](https://www.theguardian.com/football/2010/jul/09/world-cup-final-spain-pen-pics)

The archived FIFA report was downloaded, text-extracted where possible, and visually checked on its Spain formation, appearance and team-analysis pages before its claims were used.

## Changed card record

### David Silva — RESEARCH / ROLE_GUIDE

Before:

> Watch the details around Silva: moving between centre-back and full-back without being tracked. He starts his run while the back line is still watching the ball. The same idea matters when he moves wide of the centre-backs to open the middle for a teammate.

Final:

> Silva's 2010 World Cup is brief: he starts the opener on the right, roams across the edge of the box to combine with Ramos and Iniesta, and finds Villa between the lines. His only other appearance is a four-minute semi-final cameo.

Evidence and judgment: the FIFA appearance record establishes Silva's 62-minute start against Switzerland and four-minute semi-final appearance. AS's opener analysis describes his right-sided start, movement across the edge of the area, combinations with Ramos and Iniesta, and pass into Villa between the lines. The final is more honest than inventing a tournament-long off-ball identity from 66 minutes.

Limit: the role details describe the opener, not a stable pattern demonstrated throughout the tournament. The card says explicitly that the sample is brief.

### Fernando Torres — EDITION_SPLIT / ROLE_GUIDE

Before:

> To assess Torres, start with attacking the space behind defenders before it fully opens. He waits for the defender to look away before accelerating. The same idea appears when he attacks the near-post lane before the marker can turn.

Final:

> Torres gives Spain a central striker who occupies the centre-backs and opens room for Villa to attack from the left. Still below full fitness after two knee operations, he ends the tournament without scoring, and Pedro takes his starting place for the semi-final and final.

Evidence and judgment: contemporary analysis and comments from Villa and Del Bosque support Torres's central occupation and the space it opened for Villa. Match records and contemporary reporting support the two knee operations, incomplete fitness, scoreless tournament and loss of the starting place. The final is specifically Torres in 2010 rather than an unproved career-level running portrait.

Limit: occupying centre-backs can benefit several attackers. The card names Villa because contemporary exact-edition reporting makes that connection directly; it does not claim Torres created every Villa chance.

### Sergio Ramos — KEEP / PORTRAIT, then researched replacement

Before:

## Blind comparison and correction pass

Two independent reviewers received randomized old-versus-proposed pairs for Silva and Torres. Both preferred the researched versions. Silva's first proposal used the weaker verb “looks for” Villa; exact opener evidence supported the firmer and more useful final “finds Villa between the lines.” Torres's first proposal used the inferred phrase “short of match rhythm”; it was replaced by the directly supported “below full fitness.”

Ramos received a separate two-reviewer blind comparison after the team-level control read exposed his duplication of Capdevila. Both reviewers preferred the new version. A final combined blind comparison again selected the researched version for all three players. Its only caution was that Silva's opener mechanics would be too specific without match evidence; the cited AS report supplies that exact support.

No rejected draft was restored, and no unchanged control was reopened after this correction pass.

## Team-level self-critique

The set now has clearer internal roles without forcing every player into an event-heavy portrait:

- Ramos and Capdevila no longer share the same card. Ramos is the right-sided carrier and attacking outlet; Capdevila is the selective, recovery-conscious left-back.
- Busquets and Alonso remain complementary rather than interchangeable: central protection and transition control for Busquets, lateral cover and forward distribution for Alonso.
- Xavi controls circulation, Iniesta penetrates on the move, Villa attacks inside from the left, Torres occupies the centre, and Silva is described honestly as a brief opener-specific role.

The strongest result is editorial restraint. Nine controls survived despite abundant material on a famous champion. The sprint did not assume that research must produce a rewrite.

Remaining uncertainty is bounded:

- Piqué, Capdevila, Busquets and Alonso remain observational portraits rather than incident-led proof. Research found no contradiction, but video-level support is stronger than the written source trail for every micro-mechanic.
- Ramos's screen-behind-him sentence is a team-structure synthesis, not a measured coverage assignment.
- Silva's football detail comes from one start, which the card states plainly.
- The untouched generated 2010 cards still trigger one cadence-concentration warning: `two-cues` covers 12.2% of generated cards. That corpus issue predates this team sprint and was not expanded into a generator rewrite.

Spain 2010 passes the restraint gate. The workflow can continue to another bounded team-edition without changing the editorial process.
