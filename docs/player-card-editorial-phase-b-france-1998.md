## Shared exact-edition reading

France's base was a four-player defence protected by Deschamps and Petit. Blanc was the more progressive central defender and Desailly the recovery partner behind him. Lizarazu and Thuram supplied width from full-back, while Barthez controlled the space behind the line and could begin transitions directly.

Ahead of that base, Zidane was the principal playmaker. Djorkaeff moved between attacking midfield and support-striker positions. Henry attacked inward from the right during the group stage. Guivarc'h became the preferred lone striker in the knockouts, with Dugarry and Trezeguet used to start or refresh the central role.

## Blind comparison and evidence correction

Two independent reviewers received randomized old-versus-proposed pairs with exact-edition evidence constraints and no old/new labels. Both preferred all twelve initial proposals. Zidane was never presented as a rewrite because research upheld `KEEP`.

The evidence gate then rejected or narrowed several technically attractive details:

- Thuram's first draft overstated the exact sequence around Croatia's opener.
- Blanc's first draft made the partnership mechanics sound more contemporaneously measured than the sources allowed.
- Trezeguet's first draft risked specifying a touch more precisely than the evidence required.
- Desailly's old body-turn and shoulder-check cues were supportable only as generic defensive mechanics, not as visible exact-edition claims.

Desailly received one narrow alternative. Two reviewers, with A/B orientation reversed, preferred the alternative. Two fresh final reviewers then compared every changed card old-versus-final and produced a 13–0 sweep. No final card was retained merely because work had already been invested in it.

## Team-level self-critique

The final set explains one connected team. Blanc's forward licence now relates directly to Desailly's recovery role. Deschamps and Petit no longer sound like interchangeable midfield screeners. Lizarazu and Thuram both add width but in visibly different ways. Djorkaeff links the creator to the forward line, while Guivarc'h, Dugarry and Trezeguet have distinct central jobs and evidence ceilings.

The main remaining repetition is event-based: Saudi Arabia, the semi-final and the final recur often. That is defensible because France's available exact-edition evidence is concentrated in those matches and each event proves a different role. The cards avoid beginning with the same scaffold, and no incident was added solely for sentence count.

Thirteen changes from fourteen cards is unusually high and should not become a target. Four `KEEP` controls changed only after research exposed a specific unsupported or displaced identity. Zidane remaining untouched is important evidence that the process can still stop.

The correction pass also exposed three audit-model weaknesses. They were repaired without changing approved visible English:

- `back 4` now resolves to the same exact holding-midfielder evidence as `back four`.
- the word `later` counts a real second temporal beat, so Leboeuf's honest two-sentence card is not pushed toward filler;
- a context-free locale check no longer forces one player identity onto a bare surname that belongs to multiple players. This prevented David Trezeguet from being mistranslated as the later Egyptian player.

These are narrow correctness repairs, not a new enrichment or metadata framework.

## Locale review

Only the thirteen approved English changes were translated into Spanish, Korean and Chinese.

- Spanish uses natural football phrasing and the newsroom form `Trézéguet`; Petit's final run refers explicitly to the midfield of `Brasil`.
- Korean keeps roles and incidents compact. Blanc's card explicitly retains `프랑스`, and `트레제게` correctly refers to David Trezeguet rather than Mahmoud Hassan.
- Chinese preserves the same role/event boundary and uses the established nearby player forms.

The bare-surname identity collision remains a known audit tradeoff: a context-free checker now declines to enforce one localized identity for 23 genuinely ambiguous single-token aliases. Full names and all unambiguous player names remain enforced. A scope-aware current-versus-archive resolver would regain some coverage, but that complexity was not justified for this sprint.

No material translation uncertainty remains.

## Final self-critique

France 1998 is a clear net improvement and supports moving to the next team-edition. The best result is not simply more detail; it is better hierarchy. Each card now leads with the player's football identity in this side and uses a tournament incident only when it proves that identity.

The chief caution is event density. France's title run makes the final and semi-final tempting shortcuts, and future teams with thinner records must not imitate this density. The process should continue to accept concise role guides, preserve controls, and reject exact-looking mechanics that the edition sources cannot actually support.

No unresolved process wall requires approval before proceeding. No unrelated portrait, Best XI rationale, UI layout, broad generator system, commit, push or deployment was changed.
